# Final Project Description: Capstone Project

## Overview

The capstone project for the UC Law AI Bootcamp (Spring 2026) is a **3-week culminating assignment** where students demonstrate their ability to evaluate, design, or build AI-augmented legal workflows while integrating professional responsibility and strategic thinking throughout.

**Timeline**: Assigned Session 5 (March 20) → Presented Session 6 (April 10)

**Format**: Pass/fail, with emphasis on rigorous analysis and learning from both successes and failures

## Core Philosophy

The capstone emphasizes **experiential learning** over theoretical perfection. Students are encouraged to:
- Choose projects aligned with their career interests and "human edge" (unique expertise, passions, or experiences)
- Engage in hands-on experimentation with AI tools
- Integrate ethics and professional responsibility from the start
- Produce honest assessments—**"this doesn't work safely" is a valid and valuable conclusion**

## Project Structure: Two Primary Tracks

Students select an **engagement mode** based on their interests and learning goals:

### Evaluator Track
**Focus**: Audit and critique existing AI systems or workflows

**Approach**: Test, document, and rigorously assess AI tools or workflows to determine their capabilities, limitations, and ethical boundaries

**Typical deliverables**: Analysis reports, comparison matrices, purchase recommendations with risk assessments

### Builder Track
**Focus**: Design and prototype new AI-augmented tools or workflows

**Approach**: Create working prototypes (even if "vibe-coded" quickly) to demonstrate AI-enabled legal processes

**Typical deliverables**: Working prototypes with documentation, demo videos, implementation roadmaps

## Featured Option: System Alignment Challenge

The marquee project option tackles a fundamental question: **How do we align AI tools not just with "law," but with *justice*?**

This addresses two alignment problems:
1. **Micro-alignment**: The language we use to train AI shapes its "moral geometry"
2. **Macro-alignment**: The structural design of legal service delivery determines who gets justice

### Track A: The Evaluator ("Wet Cement" Auditor)

**Focus**: Embeddings, language, and moral geometry

**Task**: Conduct a "Moral Embedding Audit" of a legal workflow (e.g., eviction notices, plea offers, contract terminations) to examine how language choices affect AI reasoning and outcomes

**Deliverables**:
- Jupyter Notebook with before/after vector analysis and visualizations
- Linguistic Policy Memo (5-8 pages) arguing why specific legal language must change
- Side-by-side AI outputs using "cold" vs. "warm" terminology

**Skills developed**: Embeddings, vector similarity, data visualization, prompt engineering, statistical analysis

**Example projects**: Eviction notice bias audit, plea bargain language analysis, contract termination dignity study

### Track B: The Builder ("Alliance" Architect)

**Focus**: Service design, access to justice, and regulatory strategy

**Task**: Prototype a "Community Justice Node"—an AI tool designed for non-lawyer settings (libraries, union halls, tenant organizations)

**Deliverables**:
- Working prototype with documentation (GitHub repo)
- Service design blueprint mapping human/AI handoffs
- Regulatory strategy memo (5-8 pages) defending against unauthorized practice of law (UPL) claims
- 5-minute demo video

**Skills developed**: LLM API integration, prompt engineering, workflow design, UX for non-technical users, regulatory analysis

**Example projects**: Freelancer legal cooperative tool, tenant defense toolkit, small claims navigator

### Hybrid Option: Full Stack Alignment Architect

Build the "Alliance" tool (Track B), then run the "Wet Cement" audit (Track A) on its own internal prompts to certify it as "Justice Aligned."

**Additional deliverable**: Alignment Certification Report proving the tool's language choices align with stated values

**Why this option**: Technically rigorous, legally sophisticated, and portfolio-ready proof of capability

## Alternative Project Options

Students may choose from several practice-oriented scenarios or alternative approaches:

### Practice-Oriented Scenarios

Each scenario can be approached as either **Evaluator** (audit existing tools) or **Builder** (design new workflows):

1. **Small Firm Practice**: 5-attorney plaintiff's employment firm evaluating AI for intake and demand letters
2. **In-House Legal Operations**: First legal ops hire managing vendor NDAs and procurement contracts
3. **Access-to-Justice Tool**: Legal aid organization considering AI for underserved populations
4. **Litigation Discovery**: Mid-size firm evaluating AI-assisted document review
5. **Solo/Small Practice Automation**: Solo practitioner with limited budget seeking efficiency
6. **Appellate Practice**: Appellate clinic or practice evaluating AI for record review and research

### Other Approaches

- **Deep Ethics Dive**: In-depth analysis of emerging ethics issues (privilege in multi-tenant systems, conflicts in shared tools, defining competence for AI-augmented practice)
- **Tool Comparison Study**: Rigorous evaluation of 3+ tools on identical legal task with comparison matrix and purchase recommendation
- **Workflow Design Sprint**: Design novel AI-augmented workflow for repetitive legal task with working prototype and evaluation

## Technical Depth Flexibility

Projects can accommodate varying technical comfort levels:

- **Analysis-focused**: Evaluation, comparison, decision frameworks (requires legal research, tool testing, interview skills)
- **Build-light**: Simple workflows using existing tools and prompt engineering (requires API basics, workflow design)
- **Build-heavy**: Custom prototypes with embeddings, agents, complex workflows (requires coding, LLM APIs, technical architecture)

All depth levels are equally valid when executed rigorously.

## Evaluation Criteria

Strong projects demonstrate:

- **Rigor**: Nuanced analysis beyond "AI good/bad"
- **Professional responsibility**: Ethics integrated throughout, not as afterthought
- **Realism**: Honest cost-benefit assessment, not vendor marketing
- **Failure awareness**: What could go wrong and how to mitigate
- **Implementation thinking**: Not just "should we" but "how would we"

**Important**: Discovering "this doesn't work safely" is a valid and valuable conclusion if well-supported.

## Getting Started

1. **Choose engagement mode**: Evaluator, Builder, or Hybrid?
2. **Select scenario**: What practice context or problem interests you?
3. **Scope appropriately**: 3-week timeline with final presentation
4. **Research landscape**: What exists? What's possible? What are the constraints?
5. **Define success criteria**: What would "done well" look like for this project?

## Relation to Earlier Projects

The capstone builds on skills developed in earlier projects:

- **Project 1** (Tool Evaluation): Comparing AI tools on real tasks
- **Project 2** (Playbook Encoding): Translating human checklists to AI instructions
- **Project 3** (Workflow Design): Creating new AI-augmented processes

The capstone integrates these skills into a substantial, student-driven project that demonstrates readiness for AI-enabled legal practice.

## Note on Pedagogical Approach

This capstone reflects the instructors' shared emphasis on:
- **Experiential, hands-on learning** over lecture
- **Ethics and professional responsibility** (CA Rule 1.1 competence)
- **Practical AI tool evaluation and use**
- **Building judgment** about when AI is and isn't appropriate

The project is designed to prepare the first generation of AI-enabled lawyers who can confidently and ethically wield AI tools from day one of practice.
